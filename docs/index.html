3. Why the raw pattern is misleading

Every household plotted by year-one category breadth vs. year-two trips, colored by how often they already shopped in year one. Within a color, breadth barely moves the picture — the color is doing most of the work in the raw chart above. Rings mark the households currently classified "high value."

Reading the result

The raw relationship is large: the highest-breadth quartile has a far higher "high value" rate than the lowest. But high-breadth households were also already shopping much more — they had more opportunities to touch more categories.

After controlling for prior trip frequency and prior spend, category breadth still carries incremental signal: ≈3.6% more future trips per 10 additional year-one categories, holding baseline activity constant.

This is an association, not a causal estimate — the dataset is observational and limited to already-frequent shoppers. The natural next step is a real intervention: nudge comparable customers toward one additional relevant category and measure incremental engagement against a control group.
